\title{Compact Offset-Free Interaction-Error MPC with Applied-Torque Constraints 
for Physical Human--Robot 
Interaction}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Reference-holding physical human--robot interaction requires bounded
transient deviation followed by offset-free reference recovery, without
retaining a static deflection under sustained load. Operational-space
cancellation reduces the translational tracking error to a
\textbf{double integrator with a fixed transition matrix and a
configuration-scheduled input map}, turning physical interaction into a
predictive quantity on that representation rather than a property
re-derived per contact or configuration. Built on that representation, we present a compact
offset-free model-predictive controller for torque-controlled
manipulators that matches the reference-holding requirement under the same
physical torque limits as a stiffness-and-damping-calibrated
impedance baseline, using no force/torque sensor. A recursive Kalman
filter estimates the persistent interaction and model mismatch directly
in force coordinates, and a 30-variable quadratic program predicts the
error while constraining the complete first applied joint-torque
command. In a 1\,kHz
MuJoCo simulation of a 7-DOF Franka FR3, under a common actuator budget the
sensorless controller reaches 0.042\,mm repeated-step steady-state error at
113\,W peak positive joint power with no torque saturation, versus 2.59\,mm
at 378\,W and 0.45\% saturated samples for a stiffness-and-damping-calibrated
impedance
baseline calibrated to the same closed-loop stiffness. This is about a
third of the power in this task, with no force measurement, and an
architecturally zero-mean offset under the stated frozen-configuration
assumptions (Proposition~1). Controlled
ablation attributes the offset rejection to the disturbance-augmented
estimator: adding it to an otherwise identical MPC reduces steady-state
error from 2.77 to 0.042\,mm, consistent with the paper's frozen-configuration
offset-rejection proposition, while ideal measured-force cancellation
independently reproduces the constant-load rejection.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Impedance and admittance control provide transparent interaction behavior
\cite{hogan1985impedance,chiaverini1999survey}. Their objectives depend on the
task: in hand guiding, force-induced displacement may be the command, whereas
reference-holding and recovery require a robot to return to a desired motion
after yielding to contact. A finite-stiffness impedance retains the equilibrium
bias $p-p_d=K_d^{-1}F_h$ under a sustained human force $F_h$. Integral action
can remove this bias, but must be coordinated with actuator saturation
\cite{cao2004antiwindup,cao2002antiwindup}.

Predictive interaction control is already well established. Existing work
optimizes interaction dynamics \cite{gold2023mpic}, combines impedance and
constraint optimization \cite{chen2025unified}, learns interaction models
\cite{haninger2023model}, estimates force online \cite{jordana2024force}, or
enforces passivity and safety conditions
\cite{cao2023passive,matschek2023safe}. High-rate nonlinear manipulator MPC is
also possible with tailored computation \cite{kleff2021highfrequency}, and
combining MPC with an extended-state disturbance observer has been
demonstrated on hardware at millisecond rates \cite{wu2025ensuring}. Thus
MPC, disturbance estimation, and predictive impedance are established
ingredients; the present contribution is their combination into a compact
operational-space interface.

Two of these differ from the present formulation in model scope and sensing.
Jordana~\emph{et~al.}~\cite{jordana2024force} embed direct force-sensor
feedback in a full nonlinear whole-body MPC, estimating the discrepancy
between predicted and measured contact force within that model; the present
controller instead uses no force/torque sensor and estimates an unmodelled
aggregate disturbance as a random walk in a reduced, configuration-independent
three-dimensional force channel. Gold~\emph{et~al.}'s MPIC
\cite{gold2023mpic} explicitly predicts robot--environment interaction force
through an interaction model cascaded with low-level PD control and
optimizes a motion--force trade-off through an independently weighted
prediction term. The present controller instead treats the persistent
interaction as an unknown aggregate input and targets zero-mean reference
error under constant load through force-coordinate disturbance augmentation
and an input-centered cost, achieving offset rejection with no explicit
motion--force weighting:
$\Fmpc=-\dhat$ is exactly cost-free at any position weight. The present
contribution is therefore not online force estimation or predictive
interaction control as such, but their combination into a sensorless,
constant-transition offset-free interface with a proved frozen-configuration
equilibrium and a first-move torque-feasibility guarantee.

Offset-free MPC commonly augments a nominal model with a constant output or
input disturbance and estimates that state online
\cite{pannocchia2003disturbance}. The choice of disturbance coordinates is
important here. An acceleration disturbance changes with the robot's
configuration through the task inertia, whereas a constant Cartesian load is
approximately constant in force coordinates. We therefore place the
disturbance in the same force channel as the optimizer: this choice makes the
dominant term, the contact force itself, constant in these coordinates, so
the random-walk estimator is asymptotically unbiased for it, while the
contributions (inertial coupling, friction, model error)
are absorbed as a slowly varying residual rather than individually
cancelled. This also distinguishes
load rejection from intent inference: the estimated vector includes contact,
unmodelled dynamics, and cancellation error and cannot identify which part was
generated deliberately by a person.

Placing the disturbance in the force channel is not merely an implementation
choice but this paper's central technical claim, carried through by two
further mechanisms sharing that same channel: a centered input cost that
makes the steady balancing input $\Fmpc=-\dhat$ exactly cost-free, avoiding a
separate offset calculation, and a first-move constraint applied to the
complete torque command $\tau_{\rm base}+J_v^\top F_{\mathrm{mpc},0}$ rather
than to the corrective force alone. Operational-space cancellation exposes
the normalized translational error model these mechanisms share. Two
controlled comparisons below deliberately separate credit: \S V-A shows
that the disturbance estimate, not receding-horizon prediction, drives
offset rejection; \S V-F shows that this paper's slack-relaxed constraint
embedding, not deeper prediction, drives torque-feasibility robustness.
The contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item a \textbf{double-integrator interaction-error representation}:
operational-space cancellation reduces translational tracking error to a
fixed linear system whose input matrix is scheduled only through the
current task inertia, making interaction a predictive state on a shared
model rather than a property re-modeled per contact or configuration;
\item force-domain disturbance augmentation on that representation, with a
centered input cost that admits the steady balancing input $\Fmpc=-\dhat$
at no cost, validated by a non-predictive observer-impedance baseline;
\item a first-move applied-torque constraint on the complete torque
command, and a slack relaxation of that row that, at a practically
relevant tightened budget, recovers full tracking quality where the hard
bound fails on 72.5\% of solves; and
\item a controlled FR3 simulation comparison against nominal, calibrated,
integral, variable-impedance, and ideal measured-force baselines, including
a deliberately low-stiffness condition demonstrating transient compliance
followed by offset-free recovery.
\end{enumerate}

This conference version focuses on the implemented controller and its
causal ablation, the core experimental claim; longer proofs, parameter
sweeps, and barrier/passivity extensions are developed in the journal
version.

\subsection{Problem statement and evaluation questions}

Given $p_d(t)$, joint feedback $(q,\dot q)$, and a model
$(M,C,G,J)$, the reference-holding objective is to minimize
$e=p_d-p$ while respecting the physical joint-torque box. No contact-force
measurement is supplied to the proposed controller. We ask three concrete
questions. First, can a 3-D disturbance state remove the sustained-load bias
without changing the nominal MPC tuning? Second, does the comparison remain
meaningful after matching a classical impedance controller to the MPC's
closed-loop response and enforcing the same actuator limits? Third, how does
the flat disturbance prediction degrade under time-varying loads? These
questions determine the ablations and metrics in Section~V.

\section{Interaction-Error Model}

Consider an $n$-DOF torque-controlled manipulator; the experiments use the
Franka FR3 ($n=7$) \cite{franka2023fr3}. Its dynamics are
\begin{equation}
M(q)\ddot q+C(q,\dot q)\dot q+G(q)=\tau+J^\top(q)\mathcal F_h,
\label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal F_h$ is positive when the human pushes onto the robot. Let
$J_v\in\mathbb R^{3\times n}$ be the translational Jacobian and
\begin{equation}
\Lambda(q)=\left(J_vM^{-1}J_v^\top\right)^{-1}
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
the translational operational-space inertia \cite{khatib1987unified}. The
implementation regularizes $\Lambda^{-1}$ by $10^{-6}I$ and operates away
from singularities.

The commanded torque separates feedforward, translation, orientation, and
null-space channels:
\begin{equation}
\tau=\tau_{\rm ff}+J_v^\top\Fmpc+J_\omega^\top F_{\rm ori}
      +\Nbar^\top\tau_{\rm null},
\label{eq:torque}
\end{equation}
where $\Nbar$ is the dynamically consistent projector of the full pose task
\cite{sentis2005synthesis}. Feedforward cancellation uses
\begin{equation}
\tau_{\rm ff}=C\dot q+G+J_v^\top\Lambda\ddot p_d.
\label{eq:feedforward}
\end{equation}
With $e=p_d-p$, the residual translation is
\begin{equation}
\ddot e=-\Lambda^{-1}(q)\Fmpc+d_{\rm acc},
\label{eq:error}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\rm acc}$ collects the projected interaction wrench,
$-\dot J_v\dot q$, orientation-to-translation coupling, friction, and model
error. This cancellation-plus-disturbance structure is what keeps the
residual dynamics tractable, without requiring perfect nonlinear
inversion. We use the force-equivalent disturbance
$d=-\Lambda d_{\rm acc}$. Only the interaction-wrench contribution to $d$ is
constant under a constant translational human force, independent of
$\Lambda(q)$; the remaining components of $d_{\rm acc}$ --- the Coriolis-like
coupling term, orientation-to-translation coupling, friction, and model
error --- are themselves configuration- and motion-dependent, so the
aggregate $d$ need not be constant even when the human force is. This is
exactly why $d$ is modeled as a random walk rather than a known constant.

For error state $x=[e^\top,\dot e^\top]^\top$ and sample period $\Delta t$,
exact zero-order-hold discretization of this frozen-$q_k$ reduced predictor
(not the full nonlinear robot dynamics) gives
\begin{equation}
x_{k+1}=A_dx_k+B_d(q_k)(F_{\mathrm{mpc},k}+d_k),
\label{eq:discrete}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
A_d=\begin{bmatrix}I&\Delta t I\\0&I\end{bmatrix},\quad
B_d(q)=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\Delta t^2}{2}\Lambda^{-1}(q)\\
-\Delta t\Lambda^{-1}(q)\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:AB}
\end{equation}
Because the continuous double-integrator matrix is nilpotent, $A_d$ is exact
and configuration independent; only $B_d$ is scheduled. This constant-$A_d$
property is a feature of the reduced translational predictor, the layer
the complete robot dynamics and torque map build on.

\section{Offset-Free Receding-Horizon Controller}

\subsection{Recursive disturbance estimator}

Augment the force-equivalent disturbance as a random walk:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}x_{k+1}\\d_{k+1}\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}A_d&B_d(q_k)\\0&I\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}x_k\\d_k\end{bmatrix}+
\begin{bmatrix}B_d(q_k)\\0\end{bmatrix}F_{\mathrm{mpc},k}+
\begin{bmatrix}0\\w_k\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:augmented}
\end{equation}
A discrete recursive Kalman filter observes $[e^\top,\dot e^\top]^\top$ from
robot position and velocity feedback and propagates the $9\times9$
covariance at each QP update. Its gain is recomputed rather than frozen.
The random walk tracks constant and slowly varying aggregate loads; it does
not infer human intent or separate human force from model error.

Let $z=[x^\top,d^\top]^\top$, $C_z=[I_6\;0]$, and denote the two matrices in
\eqref{eq:augmented} by $A_z(q_k)$ and $B_z(q_k)$. The implemented recursion is
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
\hat z^-_{k+1}&=A_z(q_k)\hat z^+_k+B_z(q_k)F_{\mathrm{mpc},k},\label{eq:kfpredict}\\
P^-_{k+1}&=A_z(q_k)P^+_kA_z^\top(q_k)+Q_z,\\
S_{k+1}&=C_zP^-_{k+1}C_z^\top+R_z,\\
L_{k+1}&=P^-_{k+1}C_z^\top S_{k+1}^{-1},\\
\hat z^+_{k+1}&=\hat z^-_{k+1}+L_{k+1}
 (y_{k+1}-C_z\hat z^-_{k+1}),\label{eq:kf}\\
P^+_{k+1}&=(I-L_{k+1}C_z)P^-_{k+1}.
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Here $y=[e^\top,\dot e^\top]^\top$, with $p$ from forward kinematics and
$\dot p=J_v\dot q$. The covariance $Q_z$ contains a disturbance random-walk
block $Q_w$; $R_z$ represents the kinematic measurement channel. This filter
uses no extra numerical differentiation of end-effector velocity.

For a frozen nonsingular $B_d$, the disturbance is observable from the
measured error state because it affects $x_{k+1}$ through $B_d$; equivalently,
the first two block rows of the augmented observability matrix contain
$[I_6\;0]$ and $[A_d\;B_d]$. Since $B_d$ has column rank three away from task
singularities, no nonzero disturbance direction is hidden. This establishes
the structural requirement behind the estimator; convergence rate under
noise and model mismatch is characterized empirically in Section~V.

\subsection{QP and applied-torque constraint}

For horizon $N=10$, stack
$U=[F_{\mathrm{mpc},0}^\top,\ldots,F_{\mathrm{mpc},N-1}^\top]^\top\in\mathbb R^{30}$ and the
predicted errors $X$. With $d_N=\bm 1_N\otimes\dhat$, solve
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
\min_U\quad &\frac12X^\top\bar QX+
 \frac12(U+d_N)^\top\bar R(U+d_N),\label{eq:cost}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &-F_{\max}\le F_{\mathrm{mpc},i}\le F_{\max},\quad i=0{:}N-1,
\label{eq:forcebox}\\
&-\tau_{\max}\le\tau_{{\rm base},i}+J_{v,i}^\top F_{\mathrm{mpc},i}
\le\tau_{\max},\ i=0{:}N_c\!-\!1,\label{eq:taubox}
\end{align}
\label{eq:qp}
\end{subequations}
where $\tau_{{\rm base},i}=\tau_{{\rm ff},i}+J_\omega^\top F_{\rm ori}
+\Nbar^\top\tau_{\rm null}$. The step-0 quantities
$\tau_{{\rm ff},0},J_{v,0}$ are the exact current-configuration values; for
$i\ge1$ they are evaluated at the reference-scheduled configuration
$\bar q_i=q_d(t+i\Delta t)$ --- the best available prediction of the
future state, since the realized trajectory is not yet known --- while
orientation and null-space terms stay frozen at their step-0 value.
$N_c\in\{1,\ldots,N\}$ sets how many leading rows are enforced; the
reported experiments use $N_c=1$ (first-move only), with the
reference-scheduled extension to $N_c=N$ implemented but not separately
benchmarked here.

\emph{Slack-relaxed variant} (\S V-F): under a deliberately tightened
budget, \eqref{eq:taubox}'s $N_c=1$ row is instead relaxed to
$-\tau_{\max}-s\le\tau_{\rm base}+J_v^\top F_{\mathrm{mpc},0}\le\tau_{\max}+s$,
$s\in\mathbb R^7_{\ge0}$, penalized $\frac12\rho\|s\|^2$ added to
\eqref{eq:cost}, extending the decision variable to $[U;s]\in\mathbb
R^{37}$ within the same QP. This keeps the problem always feasible (the
force box \eqref{eq:forcebox} alone is), trading a bounded, penalized
torque-row violation for tracking; every other reported result keeps
this relaxation off.

The centered effort term in
\eqref{eq:cost}, rather than $\|U\|_{\bar R}^2$, makes the steady balancing
input $U=-d_N$ cost-free. Condensing yields
$H=\Gamma^\top\bar Q\Gamma+\bar R$ and
$h=\Gamma^\top\bar Qx_{\rm free}+\bar R d_N$. The current implementation
tests this identity directly to prevent an absolute-input penalty from
reintroducing an $R$-dependent offset.

For completeness, the condensed prediction used in code is
\begin{equation}
X=\Phi x_k+\Gamma U+D_{\rm bar}\dhat_k,\qquad
D_{\rm bar}=\Gamma(\bm 1_N\otimes I_3),
\label{eq:lifted}
\end{equation}
where the $j$th block row of $\Phi$ is $A_d^j$ and the $(j,i)$ block of
$\Gamma$ is $A_d^{j-1-i}B_d$ for $i<j$. Thus
$x_{\rm free}=\Phi x_k+D_{\rm bar}\dhat_k$ and
\begin{equation}
H=\Gamma^\top\bar Q\Gamma+\bar R,\qquad
h=\Gamma^\top\bar Qx_{\rm free}+\bar R d_N.
\label{eq:condensed}
\end{equation}
Because $\bar R\succ0$, $H\succ0$ and the optimization is a strictly convex
QP. The identity $D_{\rm bar}=\Gamma(\bm 1_N\otimes I_3)$ is also a useful
implementation check: the estimator's force must enter every prediction step
through exactly the same scheduled input map as the commanded force.

\subsection{Relation to a realized impedance}

For fixed $q$, inactive constraints, and $\dhat=0$, the strictly convex QP has
the unique solution
\begin{equation}
U^\star=-H^{-1}\Gamma^\top\bar Q\Phi x_k.
\label{eq:unconstrained}
\end{equation}
If $S_0=[I_3\;0\;\cdots\;0]$ selects the first input block, then
$F_{\mathrm{mpc},0}=S_0U^\star=K_e e+K_{\dot e}\dot e$. The unconstrained
predictive controller is therefore a configuration-dependent linear
impedance whenever the symmetric parts of $K_e$ and $K_{\dot e}$ have the
stiffness and damping sign structure. Importantly, $Q_p$ and $Q_v$ are cost
weights; they are not themselves the realized stiffness and damping. The
realized gains are the finite-horizon Riccati image in
\eqref{eq:unconstrained}.

This relation explains why a nominal 300\,N/m impedance is not automatically a
fair comparator for the tuned MPC. It also identifies what the disturbance
augmentation changes. For a constant load and exact estimate, define
$v=F_{\mathrm{mpc}}+d$. The centered cost penalizes $v$, so the same feedback
acts on the error while the physical command contains the equilibrium
feedforward $F_{\mathrm{mpc}}=-d$. In contrast, an unaugmented finite
stiffness law must retain a nonzero error to produce its balancing force.

Equation~\eqref{eq:taubox} constrains the complete torque that will actually
be applied at the current receding-horizon step, including feedforward,
orientation, and null-space terms. Because only the first move is
applied, the nominal command satisfies the magnitude limits whenever the
QP is feasible, backed by runtime magnitude clipping and torque-rate
limiting as final guards. If $\tau_{\rm base}$ alone exhausts the budget,
the released benchmark conservatively falls back to zero correction, a
safe default that does not rely on unverified recursive feasibility.

\begin{proposition}[Frozen-configuration zero-mean offset rejection]
Fix $q$, assume the augmented pair in \eqref{eq:augmented} is detectable, the
closed loop is stable, the QP constraints remain inactive almost surely over
the stationary estimation fluctuations at steady state (not only in a mean
sense, so the unconstrained affine first-move law applies to essentially
every realization), the estimator is asymptotically unbiased for a constant
$d$ (i.e. $\mathbb E[\hat d_k]\to d$), and the unconstrained first-move
position gain $K_e$ is nonsingular. Then the mean equilibrium satisfies
$\mathbb E[e_\infty]=0$ and $\mathbb E[F_{\mathrm{mpc},\infty}]=-d$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Taking expectations of \eqref{eq:discrete} at equilibrium gives
$B_d(\mathbb E[F_{\mathrm{mpc},\infty}]+d)=0$; away from singularities $B_d$
has full column rank, so $\mathbb E[F_{\mathrm{mpc},\infty}]=-d$, and the
first block row then gives $\mathbb E[\dot e_\infty]=0$. Because
$D_{\rm bar}=\Gamma(\bm 1_N\otimes I_3)$ (\eqref{eq:lifted}), $X$ depends on
$U$ only through $V=U+d_N$, so the cost \eqref{eq:cost} written in $V$ is
independent of $\hat d$ and the unconstrained law is exactly
$F_{\mathrm{mpc},0}=K_ee+K_{\dot e}\dot e-\hat d$ for every $\hat d$, not
only $\hat d=0$. Taking expectations at equilibrium, with
$\mathbb E[\hat d_\infty]=d$ by unbiasedness,
$\mathbb E[F_{\mathrm{mpc},\infty}]=K_e\mathbb E[e_\infty]-d$. Equating the
two expressions for $\mathbb E[F_{\mathrm{mpc},\infty}]$ gives
$K_e\mathbb E[e_\infty]=0$, hence $\mathbb E[e_\infty]=0$ since $K_e$ is
nonsingular.
\end{proof}
For the persistently-tuned filter implemented here ($Q_w\succ0$, held
constant online rather than decayed), the estimation error retains a
nonzero steady-state covariance even for exactly constant $d$, so realized
trajectories fluctuate around this mean equilibrium rather than converging
to it pointwise --- exactly the behavior behind Section~V's nonzero
residual floors.

This frozen-configuration result is complemented by the moving-reference
and active-constraint evaluations of Section~V. For time-varying $d$,
flat horizon prediction incurs the standard bound
\begin{equation}
\|d_{k+i}-\dhat_{k|k}\|\le e_K+L_di\Delta t,
\label{eq:predictionbound}
\end{equation}
when $\|d_k-\dhat_{k|k}\|\le e_K$ and $\|\dot d\|\le L_d$. Only the first
move is applied and the estimate is refreshed at the next solve.

\subsection{Auxiliary channels and implementation scope}

Orientation uses a separate operational-space PD channel. With axis--angle
error $e_R$ extracted from $R_d^\top R$, it applies
\begin{equation}
F_{\rm ori}=-K_{\rm rot}e_R-D_{\rm rot}\omega,\qquad
K_{\rm rot}=20I_3,\quad D_{\rm rot}=6I_3.
\label{eq:orientation}
\end{equation}
This channel's cross-coupling with translation is captured in
$d_{\rm acc}$ in \eqref{eq:error}.

For the redundant FR3 degree of freedom, define joint clearance
$\phi_i=\min(q_i-q_{\min,i},q_{\max,i}-q_i)$. Within the activation distance
$\delta_i=0.1(q_{\max,i}-q_{\min,i})$, the signed inverse-barrier gradient has
magnitude
\begin{equation}
|g_i(q)|=k_b\left(\frac1{\phi_i}-\frac1{\delta_i}\right),\qquad k_b=50,
\label{eq:softbarrier}
\end{equation}
and points away from the nearer limit. The null-space torque is
$\tau_{\rm null}=-k_{\rm null}(q-q_0)+g(q)-d_{\rm null}\dot q$. Near a task
boundary, the reference is additionally shifted by
\begin{equation}
\delta p=k_{\rm ws}(J_vJ_v^\top+\epsilon_rI)^{-1}J_vg(q),\qquad
\|\delta p\|\le0.06\ {\rm m}.
\label{eq:workspace}
\end{equation}
The circular and waypoint comparisons disable this shift; the boundary test
uses $k_{\rm ws}=5\times10^{-4}$.

The code also includes a one-step control-barrier projection and a
translational energy-tank filter, validated empirically over the
boundary and passivity scope exercised in Section~V.

The QP runs at 100 or 500\,Hz and the feedforward/realization loop at 1\,kHz.
OSQP \cite{stellato2020osqp} is warm-started and its preallocated matrices are
updated with the current $B_d$, Jacobian, and torque offset. The optimization
dimension is independent of joint count, but deployment still requires the
robot model, full Jacobian, joint limits, and actuator limits.

\section{Real-Time Realization}

The controller is implemented at two rates. At every 1\,kHz physics step, the
robot model, reference acceleration, orientation term, and null-space term are
updated. At a QP tick, the same measured state is used to update the recursive
filter, rebuild the scheduled prediction and affine torque row, and solve for
the first task-force move. That move is held until the next QP tick. The
recursive filter's predict step in \eqref{eq:kfpredict} uses this QP-solved force
directly, not a guard-adjusted value: if a runtime guard later alters the
torque actually sent to the robot, the resulting mismatch is absorbed as
apparent disturbance at the next measurement update rather than fed back
explicitly. Section~V confirms this distinction is immaterial for the
reported benchmarks, where the raw limit excess stays below the
saturation threshold throughout.

The per-tick sequence is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item compute $M,C,G,J_v,J_\omega,\Lambda$ and the measured error $y_k$;
\item predict and correct $(\hat z_k,P_k)$ using the QP-solved task-force
correction from the previous tick;
\item form $\Phi,\Gamma,D_{\rm bar}$ from the current $B_d(q_k)$ and construct
$(H,h)$ from \eqref{eq:condensed};
\item add the force box and complete first-move torque row
\eqref{eq:taubox}, warm-start OSQP, and solve;
\item assemble \eqref{eq:torque} and apply the configured runtime guards to
the torque command sent to the robot.
\end{enumerate}

measured sensor noise; the simulated position feedback is otherwise exact,

\section{Simulation Evaluation}

All experiments use the MuJoCo Menagerie FR3
\cite{mujoco_menagerie,todorov2012mujoco} at 1\,kHz with joint-torque limits
$[87,87,87,87,12,12,12]$\,Nm. A 12\,cm XZ circle is traversed in 8\,s while a
15\,N downward step is applied for 3\,s of each cycle. The MPC horizon is
$N=10$, the nominal impedance stiffness is 300\,N/m, and all reported fair
comparators use the same plant and physical torque budget.

\subsection{Estimator and update-rate ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} separates estimator and update-rate effects. C4 and
C5 have identical horizon, weights, rate, and torque interface; only the
augmented disturbance estimator differs. At 100\,Hz it reduces steady-state
error from 2.77 to 0.042\,mm. $Q_w$ is scaled by the QP period between the
100\,Hz and 500\,Hz configurations (Table~\ref{tab:params}) so the
continuous-time-equivalent process-noise density, not just the raw discrete
value, is held constant across the rate comparison; C6/C7 use the
correspondingly smaller $Q_w=2I_3$. Raising the rate mainly reduces the
contact transient in this protocol, while the estimator primarily removes
the constant-load offset; on this protocol the two effects are largely
separable.

To isolate the estimator's credit from the receding-horizon prediction's,
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} adds a non-predictive \textbf{observer-impedance}
baseline: the same Kalman estimator drives $F=K_ee+K_{\dot e}\dot e-\dhat$
with $[K_e,K_{\dot e}]$ fixed to C5's own unconstrained first-move gain
(\eqref{eq:unconstrained}, evaluated once), so no horizon is re-optimized
online. It matches C5 closely (0.52 vs.\ 0.53\,mm contact RMS, 2.44 vs.\
2.58\,mm peak) and lands between C4 and C5 on steady-state error,
confirming that the $65.8\times$ headline improvement is attributable
mainly to the disturbance estimate. \S V-F isolates the effect of
embedding and relaxing the applied-torque row under active actuation
limits, by tightening the torque budget until the two mechanisms
separate.

\subsection{Low-stiffness transient compliance and recovery}
\label{sec:lowk}

Every comparison above realizes a high closed-loop stiffness (5441\,N/m
calibrated, or C4/C5's own Riccati-image gain), so peak deflection is only
a few mm: closer to a stiff trajectory tracker with a disturbance observer
than to a controller that visibly yields to contact. We test the
architecture's other end of the design space with a deliberately low
prescribed stiffness $K_d=100$\,N/m (matching the admittance baseline's
virtual-spring stiffness), realized through a cost-shaped QP variant
that directly penalizes deviation from the prescribed law's own rollout
($H=\bar R$, $h=-\bar RU_{\rm imp}$): unlike \eqref{eq:unconstrained}'s
general Riccati image, which reports whatever gain the LQ cost happens
to realize, this shaping makes the unconstrained minimizer exactly
$\Fmpc=K_de+D_d\dot e-\dhat$ for any chosen $K_d,D_d$, with and without
the $-\dhat$ term. $D_d=2\zeta\sqrt{K_d}$ assumes a unit effective mass;
$\Lambda^{-1}$ is generally anisotropic, so $\zeta=1$ is only nominal
critical damping and is visibly underdamped here (a small sweep selects
$\zeta=3$ for clean settling).

\subsection{Restoring authority under a tightened force box}

As the force box $F_{\max}$ is tightened toward zero, the QP's corrective
authority shrinks and the commanded translation channel approaches
feedforward-only cancellation. An optional fixed-backbone
architecture (C8: $F_{\rm bb}=K_{\rm bb}e+D_{\rm bb}\dot e$ commanded outside
the QP, which then shapes only an additive bounded correction, with the
full command $F_{\rm bb}+\Fmpc$ in the torque-feasibility row) recovers
$5.5$--$7.7\times$ lower contact RMS than the default realization once
$F_{\max}\le5$\,N, via a fixed, always-on restoring stiffness; the
additive correction remains the offset-free mechanism throughout this
range, converging to feedforward alone at the limiting case $F_{\max}=0$
(full parameter sweep in the journal version).

\subsection{Active torque-constraint stress test}
\label{sec:tauactive}

Every benchmark above keeps $F_{\max}=150$\,N as the binding limit; the
applied-torque row \eqref{eq:taubox} never saturates there (raw excess
$<2\times10^{-5}$\,Nm, \S V-C). We test what \eqref{eq:taubox} costs and
buys by tightening $\tau_{\max}$ directly via a multiplicative scale $s$
and comparing four otherwise-identical 100\,Hz controllers: \textbf{C5}
(the row embedded per \eqref{eq:taubox}), \textbf{Unconstrained} (the row
omitted; the plant still clips physically per \eqref{eq:torque}, so this
tests whether the QP's own optimum matters, not whether the robot ever
exceeds $\tau_{\max}$), \textbf{Observer} (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}'s
fixed-gain baseline under the same tightened budget), and \textbf{Soft}
(the slack-relaxed row above, $\rho=1$, selected by a small sweep). A
third, more severe scale was tested and excluded from the main
comparison: there, Soft's slack under a single fixed $\rho$ grows well
beyond a physically meaningful range, a regime a scale-aware weighting
is better suited to.

\subsection{Waypoint generalization}

A second task checks whether the conclusion is specific to a periodic circle.
The end effector visits three waypoints forming a triangle. At each waypoint,
a 15\,N push in a different direction begins 0.8\,s after arrival and lasts
2\,s; the robot must then dwell within 35\,mm of the target for 1\,s. All
controllers reach all three waypoints (stiff impedance and predictive
variable impedance included), repeating the controlled estimator ablation:
at 100\,Hz, contact RMS falls from 2.2 to 0.6\,mm when the disturbance
state is enabled (C4$\to$C5); at 500\,Hz the estimator continues to reduce
contact RMS (0.8 to 0.37\,mm, C6$\to$C7). Peak error follows a different
pattern under the rate-proportional $Q_w$ scaling of Table~\ref{tab:params}:
C6's 1.0\,mm undercuts C7's 1.42\,mm, mirroring Table~\ref{tab:broad}
(full per-controller table in the journal version).

The predictive variable-impedance comparator is the same
scheduler as \S V-B (in-house, family of \cite{liu2025model,roveda2019optimal});
its 5.0\,mm result again reflects a load-dependent offset from adapting a
finite stiffness rather than cancelling the load.

The moving-reference behavior is also checked on four circle planes (XZ
sagittal, XY horizontal, YZ frontal, tilted XZ-to-XY) without
plane-specific retuning: C5 remains below 0.35\,mm free-space RMS on every
plane, versus 16--25\,mm for nominal impedance (the same stiffness
confound noted above), showing directional consistency of the scheduled
operational-space model rather than a performance ratio (full per-plane
table in the journal version).

\subsection{Time-varying interaction and horizon validity}

The random-walk model predicts $d$ as constant over the short horizon; we
test that approximation at a static target under $F_z=-12\sin(2\pi ft)$\,N
for $f\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.4,0.8\}$\,Hz, reconstructing the aggregate ground-truth
disturbance from the simulator dynamics and applied correction. Both
$e_K$ and $L_d$ in \eqref{eq:predictionbound} are estimated empirically from
this same logged trajectory (RMS current-estimate error and the maximum
observed disturbance rate, respectively), so what follows verifies the
bound's internal arithmetic is self-consistent with its own logged
inputs. Across all
five rates, the measured $N$-step prediction error stays below the bound of
\eqref{eq:predictionbound}, and contact tracking degrades gracefully with
disturbance rate, from 0.08\,mm at $f=0$ to 1.04\,mm at $f=0.8$\,Hz (full
per-rate table in the journal version). This experiment validates the
finite-horizon arithmetic for the simulator protocol, a foundation for
extending toward unmodelled contact, delay, and hardware sensing in
future work.

\subsection{Force magnitude and shape}

Repeating the circular experiment with sustained loads from 5 to 25\,N
confirms the offset claim across the tested unsaturated range: C7's
steady-state deflection stays within 0.023--0.024\,mm regardless of load
magnitude, consistent with Proposition~1's load-independent prediction,
while transient peak error scales with the step's severity (0.57 to
2.45\,mm). Varying the force profile at 15\,N shows the same transient
sensitivity from a different angle: a linear ramp nearly halves peak
error relative to a step, and a sustained sinusoid raises it, agreeing
with \S V-H (full sweep table in the journal version).

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

The proposed interface separates a behavior-coordinate request from its
robot-specific torque realization. Operational-space cancellation gives a
constant-$A_d$ error predictor; force-domain augmentation aligns the estimate,
horizon model, and balancing input; and the affine first-move constraint acts
on the complete applied torque. The C4--C5 ablation attributes a $65.8\times$
steady-state improvement to disturbance augmentation under identical MPC
tuning. Stiffness/damping calibration also shows that much of the nominal
impedance-versus-MPC gap is simply stiffness, while ideal force cancellation
confirms that offset rejection can also be reached with direct force
sensing. At 500\,Hz, disturbance augmentation improves contact RMS and
steady-state offset; the first-contact peak follows a different pattern
relative to C6 (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}), since the disturbance estimate
rises quickly after the step and briefly overcorrects before settling, so
estimator bandwidth is a tuning parameter selected jointly with the
desired transient compliance.

\textbf{Scope.} This paper establishes the fixed-base translational
reference-holding case of the fixed-$A_d$, force-coordinate representation
introduced above, with the configuration- and motion-dependent remainder
of $d$ captured by the random-walk estimator (Section~II); extending to
other embodiments and contact regimes is a natural next step.

The evidence is established in torque-controlled FR3 simulation. The
predictor lumps human force and model error, assumes accurate position and
velocity feedback, and provides a frozen-configuration offset result. The
boundary test and the optional energy tank are validated at the empirical
scope demonstrated in Section~V, with the corresponding forward-invariance
and passivity certificates developed in the journal version. By design,
the observer-impedance comparison confirms that the estimator alone
accounts for the reported offset-rejection improvement under nominal
actuation headroom, not receding-horizon prediction; \S V-F instead
credits torque-feasibility robustness to the slack-relaxed constraint
embedding, tested on the same receding-horizon policy whose hard-bounded
torque row fails -- delivering unconstrained-level tracking together with
the robot's real safety margin, via a bounded, quantified use of the test
budget's own slack; an adaptive penalty weight is a natural next step.
Hardware evaluation should compare against a canonical predictive
interaction controller under shared tuning while logging force estimates,
applied torque, solver status, safety-filter slack, and energy state.
